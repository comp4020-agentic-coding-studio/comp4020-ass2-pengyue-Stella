## The brief

> Choose a real Chinese food material and pose an inverse design problem for
> it — a specific, measurable target outcome you have not simply been given
> — then solve it by inverting one or more of the course's forward models,
> and report what confidence your answer actually deserves.

This is the course's synthesis project: it draws on whichever governing
models fit your chosen material and target, not on any one module alone.
A strong project states its target with a number and a tolerance (following
Module 12's framing), is explicit about which equation each free variable
acts on, and treats the inverted answer as a hypothesis to be tested against
real data rather than a finished specification — the same caution the
course's own module pages apply to every model they present.

## What you submit

A single PDF (2,500–3,500 words excluding references and appendices),
structured as: the target outcome and why it is measurable, the forward
model(s) selected and why they apply to your material, the inversion itself
with working shown, and a closing section stating the confidence and
limitations of the result. Supporting calculations may go in an appendix.

The final project is judged as a whole rather than against a weighted
checklist — see the marking statement above — because the scope of a good
inverse-design problem varies more than a fixed criteria table can fairly
capture across different chosen materials.
