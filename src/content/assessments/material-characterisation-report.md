## The brief

> Characterise one Chinese food material of your choosing using a model from
> Stage 1 (viscoelastic moduli, a flow model, or a thermal transition), and
> report what that model can and cannot tell you about the material.

Pick a food material not already used as a module's central example — a
different regional dish, a different starch, a different protein source is
fine, so long as you can state clearly which Stage 1 model applies and why.
The report should read as a short characterisation study: state your
research question, apply the model, and be explicit about where the model's
assumptions strain against your chosen material. This is the report where
the course's honesty rule is tested directly — a well-argued report that
says "I could not verify a specific parameter for this material, so I used
an illustrative value in its place, here is why that's still useful" is a
stronger submission than one that quietly invents a number to look precise.

## What you submit

A single PDF report (1,200–1,800 words, excluding references), including at
least one clearly labelled figure or table, and a reference list drawn from
real, verifiable sources — the same standard the course's own module pages
hold themselves to (see `/references/`).

The marking table above renders in full detail; in prose, roughly a third of
the mark is for choosing and correctly applying the model itself, a quarter
for how honestly you handle what the model does not capture, and the
remainder split between presentation and sourcing.
