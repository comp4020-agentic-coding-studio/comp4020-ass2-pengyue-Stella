## Outline

- Recap: Modules 1–2 gave us mechanical measurements (moduli, flow curves).
  This lecture adds the thermal measurement toolkit those two didn't need.
- Deriving sensible heat and latent heat of transition as two different
  costs — one raises temperature, the other pays for a change of state at
  essentially fixed temperature — before either is applied to food.
- Reading a schematic differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) trace for
  red-braised pork belly: a broad fat-melting peak and a narrower, larger
  collagen-denaturation peak, and what the area under each one means.
- Why a long simmer, not a hot flash, is what a slow, high-ΔH transition
  actually demands — setting up Module 8's point that a transition also
  has its own rate, not just a temperature threshold.
- Closing Stage 1: by the end of this lecture the course has a shared
  vocabulary for *what a material does* mechanically and thermally, before
  Stage 2 asks *why* it does that.
- What to bring to [Module 3](/sessions/03-thermal-characterisation/): no
  new tools beyond Modules 1–2 — this lecture is the last new measurement
  concept before the course turns to structure.
