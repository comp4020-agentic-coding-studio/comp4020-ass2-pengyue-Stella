## Outline

- Recap: Stages 1–3 built the vocabulary (states, moduli, flow), the
  structural models (networks, gels, emulsions) and the transformation
  models (transport, kinetics, phase transitions) that Stage 4 now puts to
  work.
- Module 10 turns "does this model look right" into a number: fitting a
  model to data, reading a residual and an R², and being honest about a
  model that fits badly rather than the one you hoped would fit.
- Module 11 builds a design space directly from Module 9's glass-transition
  work — the same physics, reframed as a map with a target region on it
  rather than a single prediction.
- This lecture's second half is a full deep-dive — **Crispness Is a
  Metastable State** — on why a crisp food is not in true equilibrium: the
  physics of the glass transition, water activity, moisture migration in
  amorphous food solids, why a crisp structure fractures the way it does,
  where this family of models breaks down, and how it connects directly to
  the Crispness Phase Diagram instrument you'll use in the Laboratory
  Portfolio.
- Module 12 then asks the question in reverse: given a target texture, which
  free variables in these models would you change, and by how much.
