## Outline

- Recap: Modules 4–5 built resistance to flow through a true network —
  crystallite bridges, then percolating protein aggregates. This lecture's
  structure resists flow without ever forming one.
- Deriving Stokes' law for creaming velocity, then asking which variable a
  cook can actually change: droplet size dominates over the density
  difference, because it enters the law squared.
- Why a long-simmered bone broth stays milky: droplet size set during
  simmering, not a change in the oil-water density gap, is what is doing
  the stabilising.
- Naming where Stokes' law breaks down for a real broth — a reminder that
  every model on this site states its own limits, not just its result.
- Closing Stage 2: Modules 4–6 gave three different structural answers
  (crystallite network, protein network, stabilised dispersion) to one
  question — what makes a food resist flow. Stage 3 asks how cooking
  changes each of these structures, starting with Module 7's transport.
