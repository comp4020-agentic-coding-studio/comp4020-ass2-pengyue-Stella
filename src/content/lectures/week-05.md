## Outline

- Recap: Module 4 built a network from starch crystallites. This lecture
  builds one from aggregating protein particles instead — same idea of
  connectivity, a different molecule doing the connecting.
- Percolation, introduced as a threshold rather than a gradient: a
  dispersion of protein particles doesn't gradually thicken into a gel, it
  crosses a critical connectivity and gels essentially at once.
- Locating the gel point with the Winter-Chambon criterion, and why the
  lecture insists on "a moment, not a texture" — the gel point is a
  structural transition you can date, distinct from how firm the gel ends
  up once fully set.
- Worked example: soy milk to tofu as the clearest kitchen case of watching
  a liquid cross its own gel point in real time.
- Bridging forward to Module 6: a structure that gels via percolation
  (protein network) versus one that resists flow without ever forming a
  true network (an emulsion) — two different routes to "won't separate."
