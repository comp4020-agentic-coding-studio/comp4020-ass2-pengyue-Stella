## Outline

- Recap: Stage 2 explained what gives a food material its structure at
  rest. Stage 3 asks how cooking changes that structure — the processes a
  cook actually controls with a stove, a timer and a lid.
- Three distinct processes, each with its own governing model, that
  together make up "cooking": heat conducting or convecting into the
  material (Module 7), a chemical reaction proceeding at a temperature-
  dependent rate (Module 8), and a material crossing a phase or state
  boundary — melting, gelatinising, vitrifying (Module 9).
- Why these are modelled separately even though they happen simultaneously
  in a real pot: each has a distinct governing equation and a distinct
  timescale, and conflating them (treating "doneness" as one number) is
  exactly the kind of unexamined claim this course asks you to unpack.
- Setting up Module 9's glass-transition framing, which Stage 4 depends on
  directly: a food crossing from a rubbery to a glassy state on cooling or
  drying is the same physical event as a food losing crispness on
  re-humidifying, examined from opposite directions.
- What this sets up for Stage 4: Modules 10–12 fit models to data and
  invert them, and every model they use is drawn from this stage.
