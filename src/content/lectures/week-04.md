## Outline

- Recap: Stage 1 gave us the language to describe *what a material does*
  (its modulus, its flow curve, its thermal signature) without yet asking
  *why* it does that. Stage 2 asks why.
- The common thread across Modules 4–6: each is a different route to the
  same outcome — a network or dispersed structure that resists flow. Starch
  gels network through crystallite junction zones, proteins network through
  covalent and physical aggregation, emulsions resist separation through
  droplet size and continuous-phase viscosity rather than a true network.
- Introducing percolation as a shared idea: a structure that only "sets"
  once enough connections exist to span the system, with a critical point
  (Module 5's gel point) rather than a gradual thickening.
- Why this stage still leans on Stage 1's tools: every claim about
  structure in Modules 4–6 is checked against a measurable consequence —
  modulus, gel time, creaming rate — not asserted from ingredient lists
  alone.
- What changes for the Laboratory Portfolio from this stage onward: model
  fitting starts to matter as much as reading a single measured value, in
  preparation for Stage 4.
