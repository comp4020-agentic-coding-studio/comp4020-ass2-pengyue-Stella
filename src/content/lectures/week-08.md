## Outline

- Recap: Module 3 gave us a transition's *heat cost*; this lecture gives a
  transition — or here, a reaction — its *rate*, and the two questions are
  distinct even when they describe the same pot.
- Building the Arrhenius equation from its two pieces: a temperature-
  independent prefactor and an exponential term carrying the activation
  energy, then reading what a *higher* activation energy actually means
  for how sharply rate responds to temperature.
- Worked comparison: Peking duck skin browning faster at a higher oven
  temperature than the temperature gap alone would predict, because
  browning's activation energy makes it unusually temperature-sensitive.
- Comparing two food reactions with different activation energies side by
  side, to show that "cook it hotter" doesn't speed up every reaction by
  the same factor.
- Setting up Module 9: a reaction proceeding at a rate is one kind of
  change during cooking; a material crossing a phase or state boundary is
  another, and the next lecture keeps them separate on purpose.
