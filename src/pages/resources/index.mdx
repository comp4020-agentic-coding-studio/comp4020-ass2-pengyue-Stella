as distinct from `/references/`, which is the full bibliography behind the
course's factual and quantitative claims.

## Supplied datasets

The Laboratory Portfolio's "two supplied-dataset analyses" component uses
two datasets built for this course, each clearly labelled as illustrative
rather than measured (see each module's own data-provenance note):

- **Tapioca-pearl oscillatory rheology** (used in
  [Module 1](/sessions/01-what-is-edible-matter/)) — storage modulus G′ and
  loss modulus G″ against time, for pearls cooked for three different
  durations, constructed to sit within published ranges for cooked starch
  gels (Steffe, 1996).
- **Broth droplet-size distributions** (used in
  [Module 6](/sessions/06-colloids-and-emulsions/)) — droplet-size
  distributions for broths simmered at three intensities, for a Stokes'-law
  creaming-velocity analysis.

Both datasets are described in full, with the values you need, on their
module pages — there is nothing further to download.

## Tools

The two [Laboratory](/sessions/) instruments — the Virtual Rheometer and
the Crispness Phase Diagram — run entirely in the browser; no separate
software installation is required to use them. For the Modelling Problem
Sets and the Material Characterisation Report, any environment that can fit
a curve and report a residual is sufficient: a spreadsheet's solver, a
scientific calculator, or a short script (Python with NumPy/SciPy, or
equivalent) all satisfy the brief. The course does not require or endorse
one specific tool.

## Further reading, by topic

A short, topic-grouped starting point — `/references/` gives the complete
list with full citation detail:

- **Rheology and flow behaviour** — Steffe, *Rheological Methods in Food
  Process Engineering*; Walstra, *Physical Chemistry of Foods*.
- **Gels, networks and emulsions** — BeMiller & Whistler (Eds.), *Starch:
  Chemistry and Technology*; McClements, *Food Emulsions*; Winter &
  Chambon on the gel point.
- **Transport and kinetics** — Singh & Heldman, *Introduction to Food
  Engineering*; Damodaran & Parkin (Eds.), *Fennema's Food Chemistry*.
- **Glass transitions and stability** — Roos, *Phase Transitions in
  Foods*; Williams, Landel & Ferry (WLF); Gordon & Taylor.
