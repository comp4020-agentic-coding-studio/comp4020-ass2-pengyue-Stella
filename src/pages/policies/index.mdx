  The course-wide rules for late work, extensions, academic integrity and
  getting help.

These rules apply to all four assessments; each assessment's own page states
its due date and weight, and defers to this page for everything else.

## Late submission

Work submitted after the deadline loses 5% of the available mark per day
(or part-day) late, up to five days, after which it is not accepted without
an approved extension. This is calculated from the mark actually achieved,
not from a fixed maximum — a strong report submitted two days late is still
worth submitting.

## Extensions

Request an extension from the course convenor before the deadline wherever
possible, stating the new date you're asking for. Extensions granted for
illness, bereavement or an equivalent circumstance are not penalised; a late
request made after the deadline has passed is considered only where the
circumstance made an earlier request genuinely impossible.

## Academic integrity

Submitted work must be your own analysis, written in your own words, with
every source cited. This applies with equal force to text, code, figures
and any numeric result you report. Two rules specific to this course:

- **Cite honestly, and only real sources.** Every citation on the course's
  own module pages was checked against a real publisher or journal record
  before publication (see `/references/`); the same standard applies to
  your submitted work. An invented citation is treated as a fabrication,
  not a formatting error.
- **Label what a value actually is.** If a number in your submission is
  measured, taken from a cited source, or an illustrative estimate you
  constructed, say which. This mirrors the course's own data-provenance
  rule (see any module's "Data provenance" note) and is marked as part of
  scientific honesty, not an optional flourish.

## Use of generative AI tools

You may use a generative AI tool to explain a concept, check your working,
or help debug a fitting script, in the same way you might use a textbook or
a classmate. You may not have such a tool produce the analysis, written
argument, or numeric results you submit as your own — the Laboratory
Portfolio and Material Characterisation Report in particular are assessed
on your own reasoning about a model's fit to a specific material, which an
unexamined AI-generated answer does not demonstrate. If in doubt, ask
before submitting, not after.

## Getting help

For a question about a specific module or piece of marked work, start with
the teaching team listed on that module or assessment's own page — see
[People](/people/) for consultation hours and the escalation path. For
matters outside the course (wellbeing, disability access, hardship), use
your enrolling institution's central student support service; the
convenor can point you to the right one on request.
